Two setups, and when each one wins

The box runs one setup at a time. We start the test on Setup B. Both passed every job we gave them; they differ in speed, depth and how far each conversation can go.

Setup A

One big Ornith

Ornith-1.5-122B · 10B of 122B active per token
  • Smarter. On its makers' coding tests it scores 76.9 on SWE-bench Pro, against 59.6 for the 35B.
  • The big model everywhere. The orchestrator and every local sub-agent are the 122B.
  • Proven with six people at once: 22 of 22 team jobs passed, and nothing had to queue.
  • Slower. Replies come at about 30 tokens a second, and a fresh 1M-token read takes about an hour.
Pick it whenthe hard part is getting it right: planning, tricky bugs, reviews, long careful sessions.
Setup B Start here

The fast pair

Ornith-1.5-35B · 3B of 35B active per token
+ Qwen3.8-27B TURBO · dense, all 27B active per token
  • Fast. Ornith-35B replies at about 79 tokens a second, 2.6 times the 122B.
  • Quicker long conversations. A follow-up at ~1M tokens starts in 34 seconds, not 93, and a fresh 1M read takes about 25 minutes.
  • A fast second model. Qwen3.8-27B TURBO, a fine-tune by DavidAU trained to think in far fewer tokens, so the jobs it takes on finish quickly. Its creator removed its safety refusals. It replies at about 18 tokens a second and handles up to 262k tokens.
  • Fast team jobs: with 1 to 3 people at once, 7 of 7 team jobs passed and averaged 11 to 16 minutes, with nothing queued. We are measuring 4 to 6 people now.
  • Smaller models. Ornith-35B scores 59.6 on SWE-bench Pro against the 122B's 76.9.

    Fine printSo nothing surprises you

    What to expect

    Planning

    The orchestrator thinks first

    On a big job Ornith planned for 2 to 4 minutes on the 35B and about 7 minutes on the 122B before handing anything out. After that most of its turns took seconds.

    Sharing

    More people, slower jobs, none dropped

    On Setup A, team jobs averaged 27 to 40 minutes from one to six people at once, and every job passed. On Setup B, jobs averaged 11 to 16 minutes with up to three people. Nothing ever had to queue.

    Long context

    Big conversations stay usable after the first read

    The box remembers what it has already read, so a follow-up turn near a million tokens starts in about 30 seconds on Setup B and about 90 on Setup A. Reading a huge conversation in from scratch takes 25 to 60 minutes.

    Fair use

    Huge new prompts slow everyone

    While the box reads in a very large new prompt, everyone else's replies nearly stop until it's done. If you're about to paste in hundreds of thousands of tokens, give the group a heads-up.

    Content

    One model has no safety filter

    Qwen3.8-27B TURBO in Setup B had its refusals removed by its creator, so it will answer requests the other models decline. What you ask it is your responsibility.

    Privacy

    Your code stays yours

    We log who used which model and how many tokens. We don't store your prompts or the replies.

    Beta

    Things will change

    We may switch between the two setups during the test, and we'll warn you before any planned downtime. Tell us what breaks: that's the point.

    How the mix works

    This is the team we tested as Setup B: Ornith-35B runs the session and hands work to TURBO Qwen sub-agents. Swap the two names if you'd rather have Qwen lead.

    "CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL_FORCE": "1" makes the sub-agent entry win over an agent's own model: setting. Leave out both sub-agent entries to let each agent choose instead: its opus, sonnet or haiku choice is mapped onto our models by the three alias entries. Inside a session, /model qwen3.8-27b-turbo-anthropic switches the main model.

    Keep "ENABLE_TOOL_SEARCH": "false". Claude Code's tool search sends a block type made for Anthropic's own servers; ours now accept it, but with tool search off Claude Code skips that extra lookup. If you ever see "Unexpected item type in content", add "CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_EXPERIMENTAL_BETAS": "1".

    We tested this mix with Claude Code 2.1.317. Mixing works within one setup. In Setup A only the 122B runs, so every name in this file is answered by it or is offline. Qwen sub-agents stop at 262,144 tokens even when the main session goes to 1M. Everything goes through this address, so cloud Claude models can't join the same session.

    Bring TechLabs into your company's Claude

    Two ways to give staff our models next to Claude. Every person uses their own TechLabs key, so usage and budgets stay per person.

    Claude app: the TechLabs connector

    claude.ai, desktop and mobile · Team and Enterprise

    An Owner adds it once

    1. Open Organization settings → Connectors, click Add, then Custom.
    2. Name: TechLabs. URL: https://llm.technicallabs.org:8443/techlabs/mcp
    3. For the OAuth client, choose Register automatically, then Add.

    Each staff member connects

    1. Open Customize → Connectors, find TechLabs and click Connect.
    2. Our sign-in page opens. Paste your personal TechLabs key (ask Nicholas for one) and click Connect.
    3. In a chat, turn it on under + → Connectors.

    New this week: we have tested every step from our side. If connecting fails, send Nicholas the error text. Claude stays in charge of the conversation and calls our models as tools: list_models shows what's running, ask_model sends a prompt. Each answer has a 200-second limit, so ask for focused pieces of work. On Pro or Max, add it yourself under Customize → Connectors → Add custom connector.

    Claude Code through our LiteLLM gateway

    every request goes to TechLabs · your normal Claude keeps working

    Our gateway is LiteLLM, and it speaks Claude Code's own protocol. Give each developer the settings file from Connect with their own key, then:

    # our models: Ornith-35B leads, TURBO Qwen sub-agents
claude --settings ~/.claude/techlabs.json

# one-off job without opening a session
claude --settings ~/.claude/techlabs.json \
  -p "Add tests for src/parser.py and run them"

# your usual Claude, untouched, in another terminal
claude

    Admins can see each person's usage and set budgets per key. Anthropic doesn't officially support Claude Code on non-Claude models; we test every Claude Code version we recommend (currently 2.1.317) and turn off the one feature our models can't handle (tool search).
